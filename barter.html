UX Case Study

Of Lendings and Borrowings: Barter

A mobile peer-to-peer sharing ecosystem designed to simplify lending and borrowing of item access inside college campuses.

Role Product Designer (Research, Visuals, Interaction Flow)
Team 2 UX Designers (Saumyaa Suneja & Reuben Abraham)
Platform iOS Mobile Application
Date July 2020
01 / Concept

Introduction & Team

Why buy items you only need for a short period of time? And why leave unused items gathering dust in a dorm room? In a college campus of 6,000 coexisting people, someone has what you need, and someone wants what you have. Barter was designed to facilitate and secure peer-to-peer item exchange within the NIT Rourkela campus.

This project was designed over a two-week sprint by a close-knit team of UX designers, Saumyaa Suneja and Reuben Abraham, mapping campus challenges to core design systems.

02 / Research

Survey & Brainstorming

We circulated a Google Form over campus channels and conducted random interviews with students, researchers, and staff. We split users into target groups (Borrower and Lender) and linked pain points directly to their goals:

Core Concerns

  • Borrowers: Difficulty finding who owns what, estimating durations, trusting strangers, and handling out-of-stock items.
  • Lenders: Worrying about item damage/theft, tracking return status, and dealing with late returns.
03 / Solutions

Core Features & Trust

We designed the system around these core mechanics:

  • Direct Messaging: Users coordinate exchanges and deposits via secure chat.
  • Duration Extension: Borrowers can request an extension, subject to lender approval.
  • Item Wishlists: If an item isn't listed, users can publish a request and get notified if someone registers it.
  • Waitlists: Clear visibility of a user's position in line for highly-requested items.

Securing the Exchange

  • Lender Security: Borrowers pay a temporary security deposit. If returned damaged, a portion is sent to the lender.
  • Borrower Protection: Lenders listwear-and-tear upfront. Video handovers document condition.
  • Reciprocity Rule: Users must list at least 3 items to continue using the service after their first borrow.
  • Single Sign-On: Verified login using campus roll numbers prevents spam accounts.

06 / Styling

Design System

To project reliability and safety, we utilized a clean interface with blue accents. Blue represents trust, loyalty, and calmness—helping users feel secure about exchanging high-value items.

Typography
