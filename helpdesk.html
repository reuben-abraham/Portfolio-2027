UX Case Study

Building Disney+ Hotstar’s Helpdesk

Designing a self-resolving, robust customer support system for a consumer base of 300 million monthly active users.

Role Sole Product Designer (End-to-End Ownership)
Team 1 Product Manager, Support Leads, Engineering
Platform Mobile App (Android & iOS) / Web
Date September 2021
01 / Challenge

Background & Objective

As India's largest streaming service, Disney+ Hotstar caters to over 300 million monthly active users. To handle this size, a robust and functional customer support platform is essential. The goal was to build a system enabling users to resolve issues on their own or reach out to support, thereby improving customer satisfaction (CSAT) and reducing the support team's load.

High-Level Objective

To build a robust and functional customer support platform that would enable users find solutions to their issues in the easiest way possible and thus reduce burden on the customer support team.

02 / Research

Research & Learnings

Analyzing the Data:

  • Google Trends showed twice as many online searches for "Hotstar contact" compared to "Netflix help" (which had a dedicated portal).
  • 18.5% to 25% of the total weekly complaints raised were self-resolvable.
  • Almost 5 times more complaints were received from paying subscribers than free users.

Understanding Potential Users & Support Team:

We conducted interviews with users who raised complaints, as well as the support staff handling the bulk of weekly tickets.

The "Lost Account" Login Problem

A major source of frustration occurred when paying users lost access to their accounts. When Hotstar shifted to phone number login by default, many users who had previously signed up with email/passwords ended up accidentally creating a new free account instead of migrating. They became frustrated when asked to pay again.

03 / Information Architecture

Solving for Content

A major challenge was restructuring the complex help center contents. We grouped 12 categories down to 4 broad segments, and later iterated to design a 6-section system with an upfront "Top Categories" drawer.

04 / Interface Design

Intelligent Search & Suggestions

Universal Search Bar: Designed to parse error codes directly. If a user encounters "Error 102", entering it in search returns the exact troubleshooting article, accompanied by instant queries right below the input.

Related Articles & Queries

Recommended similar guides and top queries at the bottom of search results to simplify discovery and keep users in the self-help loop.

Lost Account Retrieval Portal

A self-serving portal where users submit verification documents to quickly recover their registered paid account without human agent intervention.

Context-Based Help Links

When an error card pops up inside the main app, a direct, deep link to the corresponding helpdesk article is shown, leading to instant self-resolution.

05 / Support Balance

Managing Support Tickets

To prevent the support team from being flooded, we structured a "Need More Help" funnel: placing contact buttons at the bottom of articles, encouraging users to browse before opening tickets.

Contact Channels Split

Separated traffic by routing users to WhatsApp or Phone Call dynamically, helping resolve simple requests via messaging and complex ones via direct calls.

Bogus Filtering & Nudges

Paid subscribers are connected to phone support, while free users are routed to email tickets. During ticket creation, relevant self-help articles are displayed dynamically as the user types.

06 / Post-Launch Iterations

Segmented Issue Bucketing

To help agents categorize incoming tickets, we built a progressive, segmented issue picker to choose 1 item out of 125 options without screen clutter.

Refinements & Localization

We discovered many support users came from Tier 2 cities and preferred regional languages, so we introduced full localization for Indian regional languages.

07 / Results
